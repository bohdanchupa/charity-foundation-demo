10 October: what we say at open meetings, and why exactly that

For World Mental Health Day the foundation held seven open meetings in communities across Lviv and Zakarpattia oblasts.

2025-10-10

Seven meetings, 340 participants

Around 10 October the team ran seven open psychoeducation meetings — in libraries, schools and community spaces. 340 people attended in total; the largest meeting drew 78, the smallest 19.

Why psychoeducation rather than a call to seek help

The most common reason a person does not seek help is not a lack of information about where to go. It is the belief that their condition is "not bad enough", or that help is for people who have "broken". A call to "reach out" does not change that belief. So the meetings are built differently: we explain how the response to a traumatic event works, why insomnia, irritability and withdrawal are symptoms rather than character traits, and at what point they stop being a normal reaction and become a condition worth treating.

The three questions asked every time

"Will it pass on its own?" Often, yes. In most people an acute stress reaction subsides within a few weeks. If after a month nothing has changed, or it has got worse, that is a different story.

"What if I start talking and feel worse?" A temporary worsening at the start of therapy does happen; it is a predictable part of the work that a specialist warns about in advance, not a sign that something went wrong.

"Will my employer find out?" No. The foundation passes on information about an approach to nobody, except where the law expressly requires it.

Materials

The handouts from these meetings — cards listing the signs of a condition that needs help, and the list of 24/7 lines — are given to communities free of charge. Requests go to the address in the contacts section.
