Online support: the first line went live for five oblasts

The foundation launched online consultations for communities with no psychologist within reach — initially five oblasts, with a waiting time of up to five days.

2023-04-11

The problem

In de-occupied and front-line communities there is often no psychologist at all, and the nearest specialist is 60–120 kilometres away, reachable by transport that runs twice a day. For a person in a difficult state that distance is insurmountable.

What we did

Since 11 April 2023 the foundation has accepted requests for online consultations from every community in five oblasts. Sessions run over video or, where the connection is unstable, by voice alone at a lower bitrate. That is a deliberate choice: requiring stable video would exclude exactly the people the programme exists for.

The format is up to eight sessions with an assigned specialist, followed by an assessment and a decision to continue, close, or refer on.

What the first months showed

  • The queue. Average wait for a first consultation: 4.8 days. We had planned for three and underestimated demand.
  • Camera off. 38 % of clients deliberately work without video. For many it is the condition on which they agree to speak at all.
  • Time of day. Requests peak between 20:00 and 23:00, once children are in bed and the housework is done. The specialists' rota had to be rebuilt around it.

Limits we state plainly

Online work is not suitable for acute crises, for cases with a risk of self-harm and nobody present, or for younger children. In those cases the foundation refers to in-person services and to the 24/7 helplines rather than trying to carry the case remotely.
