Privacy policy

Who is responsible for this site, what the forms send, where it goes, and what the site does not store.

Effective from 28 September 2026. This page describes this website specifically — what it does with the data a visitor types into its forms — not the foundation's general policy on personal data in its work with beneficiaries (that is a separate document).

Who is responsible for the data (controller)

UNIQ International Charitable Foundation (БО «Міжнародний благодійний фонд ЮНІК» — the Ukrainian name is the authoritative one). The registration code, address and an official contact for questions about this page or your own data will be added once officially confirmed, on the Contacts page. How the Foundation handles programme participants’ personal data is set out in its privacy and personal data policy.

What the site's forms send

Every form on the site (get-help, careers, procurement, partnership, report a concern) sends exactly what the person types into it: a name, a contact (email or phone), a message and, where relevant, a file (CV, proposal). The hidden anti-bot honeypot field carries none of your real data — it exists only to filter out automated submissions.

How your submission is handled

Every form works the same way: it is checked by Cloudflare Turnstile (to filter out bots), and a function hosted on Netlify then forwards the form's contents to the foundation by email through Resend. That is the only thing the form does — there is no database that submissions are written to.

As of this version of the site, that is not switched on yet: the forms run in a demo mode, and pressing "Send" does not send anything anywhere — it only shows a notice that online requests are not accepted yet. This is stated in the form itself, not only here.

What is stored, and what is not

The site (and the api/submit.ts code that accepts form submissions) has no database — no form submission is ever written down permanently; it simply continues on as an email and ends up only in the recipient's (the foundation's) mailbox, subject to ordinary mailbox retention. Two technical exceptions are worth stating plainly:

  • Spam protection. To limit how many submissions one address can send per minute, the server briefly (around 60 seconds) holds the sender's IP address in memory only — never written to disk or a database — and forgets it once that window passes.
  • Bot check. That same IP address is passed to Cloudflare Turnstile as part of confirming a submission came from a person, not a bot. This happens on every form submission once the forms are live.

The one thing stored on a visitor's own device is an in-progress draft of an unfinished form, in the browser's localStorage (not a cookie). It only operates once the forms are live — no draft is saved at all otherwise. The draft exists so an accidental page reload doesn't lose what was typed, is removed automatically after a successful send, and never leaves the browser.

Legal basis for processing

Under Article 11 of the Law of Ukraine "On Protection of Personal Data" and Article 6 GDPR (for visitors from the EU), each form relies on the following basis:

  • Get-help, careers, procurement, partnership. Your separately given consent (the checkbox under the form, labelled "I consent to my data being processed to handle this request"). The get-help form may involve health or psychological information — a special category of data — so that specific form relies on explicit consent (GDPR Art. 9(2)(a)), not ordinary Art. 6 consent.
  • Report a concern. This form has no consent checkbox — a deliberate choice, so an anonymous report stays possible. Instead, the foundation processes what is submitted under its legitimate interest in the safety of people who interact with it (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)), and, where someone's life or health may be at risk, to protect that person's vital interests (GDPR Art. 6(1)(d) and Art. 9(2)(c) for any special-category data involved).

Cookies

This site sets no cookies. The form draft described above lives in the browser's localStorage — that is not a cookie, is never sent to a server, and is not read by the site on a return visit from a different device.

Analytics

The site may use Plausible Analytics, a visit-statistics service that, by its own design, sets no cookies and collects no data that identifies an individual visitor. If analytics is not configured, no requests to it are made at all.

Hosting and IP addresses

This is a static site hosted on GitHub Pages. The foundation keeps no server logs of its own, but GitHub, as the hosting provider, may log visitor IP addresses under its own practice — that is outside the foundation's control; see GitHub's own privacy policy for details.

The map (OpenStreetMap)

The contacts page can show a map from OpenStreetMap. It does not load automatically — the map appears only once you press the "Load the map" button; no request reaches OpenStreetMap before that. Once pressed, OpenStreetMap, like any third party, receives your IP address and browser information. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation's own privacy policy for details.

Protecting children's data

The foundation works with children among others, but the site's forms are meant for adult visitors, professionals, or a child's representative. Please do not enter a child's personal data into a form yourself on the child's behalf — if the matter concerns a child, reach out as an adult representative (parent, guardian, professional) or use the crisis lines in the site footer, including the National Child Helpline.

Your rights

Under the Law of Ukraine "On Protection of Personal Data" (for requests from Ukraine) and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR — for visitors from the EU), you have the right to find out whether your data is being processed, obtain a copy of it, request correction or erasure, object to processing, and withdraw any consent previously given. To exercise any of these rights, use the Foundation’s official contact once it is published on the Contacts page. If the foundation's response does not satisfy you, you may contact the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights (for requests from Ukraine) or your own country's data protection authority (for visitors from the EU).
