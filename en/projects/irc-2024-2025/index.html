Psychological help for children and caregivers, with the IRC

Online consultations for children in alternative and family-based care and their caregivers, plus a ten-day recovery camp for families separated by the war.

Completed

Donor
International Rescue Committee
Period
2024-11-01 — 2025-09-30
Geography
  • Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia, Donetsk, Odesa, Kharkiv, Sumy, Mykolaiv, Kherson and Kyiv oblasts
  • Recovery camp in Lviv oblast
Sector
Health / Mental Health, Child Protection, Protection
Programme

Goal

450 war-affected Ukrainians aged 8 to 75 each receive ten free online consultations: 250 children in alternative or family-based care and 200 caregivers. The 50 participants with the greatest needs attend a recovery camp.

The project began in November 2024. It was extended to the end of September 2025 at no extra cost.

Who received help

  • 451 people: 339 women and girls, 112 men and boys.
  • By age: under 5: 2 · 5–14: 140 · 15–18: 59 · 19–49: 240 · 50 and over: 10.
  • Local residents: 234 · internally displaced: 169 · returnees: 48.
  • 24 people with disabilities.
  • Routes in: 184 people were referred by the IRC and 267 applied directly through the platform. The original plan was 350 and 100; the ratio was revised with the IRC during the project.

The most common reasons for referral were psychological distress (346 people), separation or lack of an accompanying adult (49), a serious health condition (15), experience of violence (14), disability (14), and the effects of mines, explosive ordnance or attacks (10).

The target for people was met; the target for consultations was not. The table shows why: a slow start. How that was fixed is described below.

The recovery camp

June 2025, Lviv oblast, ten days. Participants were 17 mothers aged 27–60 and 33 children aged 3–16 (17 girls, 16 boys). Every family had been separated by the war.

Each participant had about 30 hours with psychologists. The programme included group and individual sessions, play-based practice, body-oriented therapy, art therapy, physical activity, psychoeducation and excursions.

Feedback: 43 responses, spoken and written. 88% were positive, 9% were suggestions and 3% were technical comments. The suggestions were acted on during the camp itself: activities were split by age group and a quiet zone was set up. Feedback was collected through a QR-code form and anonymous boxes.

The IRC carried out a monitoring visit to the camp.

Feedback on the consultations

69 responses came in over the whole project, 97% of them positive. Two people asked to change specialist, and both requests were granted.

Child safeguarding

Internal child-safeguarding procedures were updated at the start of the project. A referral mechanism was developed with the IRC. Feedback channels: a website form (named or anonymous) sent after the application and after each consultation, a form on the main website, and a 24/7 line. At camp, there were also anonymous paper questionnaires and a feedback box.
