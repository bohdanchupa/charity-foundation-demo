What did not work

Everything the foundation planned and did not deliver, delivered worse than expected, or had to redo. With the figures, and with what was done about it.

Why this page exists

An annual report in which everything succeeded tells you nothing. A donor who has read a dozen of them cannot tell an organisation that works well from an organisation that writes well.

So we publish the other side. Every item below has three parts: what was planned, what happened, what was done about it. The third part is the point — without it this is not accountability, it is self-flagellation.

Every figure is reconciled with the annual reports and financial statements published under Impact.

The online waiting time did not come down

Planned. An average wait of 3 days for a first consultation.

Actual. 4.8 days in 2023, 4.2 days in 2025. The target was missed in every year.

What was done. We accepted that the cause is not queue management but demand running ahead of forecast: 4,260 consultations in 2025 against a planned 3,000. We over-delivered on volume and under-delivered on access, and those are not the same as success. The 2026–2027 scale-up targets 2.5 days, and for the first time the target is backed by a dedicated budget line for recruiting twelve specialists rather than by optimising the existing ones.

As of August 2026, eight of the twelve are recruited. The risk we named in the application has materialised: there are fewer qualified trauma therapists on the market than every currently expanding organisation needs.

The effect of the rehabilitation rotations decays at six weeks

Planned. Reduced anxiety scores in 65 % of children at the end of a rotation.

Actual. 71 % — the target was exceeded. But measurement six weeks after the return showed the result held in only 58 % of children. Among adults it is worse: exhaustion scores fell for 63 % at the end and for 41 % at six weeks.

The most durable effect is in the 7–11 age band. Among 14–16-year-olds scores return to baseline most often.

What was done. This is the most important thing we have measured, and it means ten days give a push rather than a completed treatment. From 2027 the programme adds three online follow-up sessions per family over the two months after the return, with a dedicated indicator on retention.

We are not hiding the uncomfortable consequence: earlier rotations ran without follow-up, and part of their effect was temporary.

The permanent space for the veterans programme did not open

Planned. To open in 2025.

Actual. It did not. We found no premises meeting the accessibility requirements within the rental budget.

What was done. We did not lower the requirements. A step-free entrance and an accessible washroom remain a condition, not a preference. A space a person using a wheelchair or crutches cannot enter unaided is not a support space for veterans. The search continues; in 2026 the groups meet in partner organisations' rooms.

The feedback system went live seven months late

Planned. Launch in March 2025.

Actual. October 2025. As a result the 2025 data is incomplete: feedback reaches 54 % of beneficiaries, not all of them.

What was done. We recorded in the annual report that the 2025 satisfaction figures are not representative, and we do not cite them as evidence in applications. The first full year of data will be 2026.

No external audit was carried out for 2024

Planned. An annual independent audit.

Actual. No audit was carried out for 2024: there was no funding for it. No programme grant covers that line.

What was done. In 2026 we obtained an institutional grant that funds the audit separately, and the 2025 audit was carried out — the opinion is unmodified. But a year without an audit stays in the foundation's history, and we do not pretend otherwise.

Clients disappear between mobile team visits

Planned. A visit once a fortnight to eight communities in Kharkiv oblast.

Actual. The schedule held — 46 field days and 612 consultations in three months. But a fortnight is too long for someone in an acute state, and a share of clients do not make it to the next visit.

What was done. The partial answer is online sessions in between, but that only works where there is a stable connection and somewhere to speak alone. 71 people were moved to that follow-up. In the second half of the year we are testing a weekly schedule in the three busiest communities.

The veterans pilot: the sample is small

What it showed. Retention to the sixth meeting: 74 % in groups led by a pair (veteran facilitator plus psychologist) against 41 % where a specialist led alone.

What it does not prove. Six groups over ten months is a small sample, allocation was not randomised, and there is no control group in the strict sense. The gap was enough to change the model and not enough to call it proven.

What was done. The wording in the 2025 report was corrected: it previously read "the model is confirmed". It now says measurement continues.

Also unsuccessful: we failed to retain one of the two facilitators, who stepped out of the role after six months. The pilot made no provision for their regular supervision. In the full stream it is mandatory — we made that mistake once.

Psychoeducation meetings in the community: 6 of 8

Planned. Eight meetings for local professionals — teachers, social workers, medics.

Actual. Six as of September 2026.

What was done. Nothing dramatic: these groups are only available in certain windows and the schedule had to bend. The rest will run before the project closes. It is listed so the list is complete rather than only dramatic.

Administrative costs rose from 11 % to 14 %

What happened. The administrative share in 2025 was 14 % against 11 % in 2024.

Why. We hired a finance director and a safeguarding focal point.

Why we do not regard it as a deterioration. Without those two posts the foundation fails an institutional donor's due diligence and therefore does not receive programme funding. We took the decision deliberately and we are not hiding the figure by regrouping the lines.

An honest caveat: if your assessment method excludes organisations with administrative costs above 12 %, we do not clear that bar, and that is a legitimate reason not to fund us.

Complaints in 2025

Four reports were received: two about staff conduct, one about accommodation at a rotation, and one found to fall outside the scope of the foundation's policies. All were handled within the stated timescales.

We publish this number every year, whatever it turns out to be. Zero complaints in this kind of work would mean the channel is not working, not that there is no misconduct.

What is not here

This page covers what we measured. It does not cover what we did not measure, and our largest such gap is this: we do not know what happens to people who approach us and never reach a first consultation. Their outcome is recorded nowhere, and we cannot say how many found help elsewhere and how many did not.

It is the most important question we cannot yet answer.
