About the foundation

International charity foundation: online psychological support, a mental health centre in Lviv, and rehabilitation camps in the Carpathians for children, parents and veterans.

Mandate

We are an international charitable foundation restoring the mental health of people affected by the war in Ukraine and supporting their mental and physical rehabilitation.

Vision

A Ukraine where a person who has lived through the war gets qualified psychological help when they need it — regardless of where they live or what they can pay.

Mission

To make psychological help reachable: online, where there are no specialists; in person, at the mental health centre in Lviv; and through rehabilitation rotations for children, parents and veterans in the Carpathians.

Values

  • A person is the subject of their recovery, not the object of aid. The beneficiary sets the pace and the scope of the work.
  • Do no harm. Every activity is designed to avoid re-traumatisation.
  • Confidentiality. A request and its content are not shared with third parties without the person's consent.
  • Free of charge. The foundation's services are free for beneficiaries.
  • Non-discrimination. Help is provided regardless of sex, age, ethnicity, religion, disability or displacement status.
  • Safeguarding and PSEA. All work follows the foundation's safeguarding and PSEA policies.

Programmes

  1. Online psychological support for Ukrainians affected by the war.
  2. Psychological support at the mental health centre in Lviv.
  3. Psychological rehabilitation in a Carpathian camp for children and parents.
  4. Psychological rehabilitation for veterans in Carpathian camps, combined with physical rehabilitation.
  5. Building a permanent mental and physical rehabilitation space in the Carpathians.

What is already in place

  • technology for delivering the services;
  • technology for receiving and processing applications;
  • a team experienced in building and running psychological rehabilitation projects;
  • an international charitable foundation as the legal entity behind the work.

Who we serve

Internally displaced people, children and their parents or caregivers, veterans and their families, and survivors of gender-based violence.

Accountability

The foundation operates under its own protection and anti-fraud policies and a code of conduct, all published under Policies and documents. That page also explains how to raise a complaint or give feedback, including anonymously.

How the foundation came about

Uniq was registered on 18 May 2022 — three months after the start of the full-scale invasion. Its core was formed by psychologists already working with displaced families on a voluntary basis, who saw that demand exceeded what any volunteer initiative could carry.

For the first eighteen months the foundation had no premises of its own: consultations took place in rooms lent by partner organisations, and online sessions ran from the specialists' homes. The Lviv centre opened in March 2024; the online stream started in 2023, the veterans stream in 2025, and in 2026 came the mobile team in Kharkiv oblast and the first permanent community space.

How decisions are made

The foundation has a five-member supervisory board that approves the strategy, the annual budget and the reporting, and selects the external auditor. Day-to-day management rests with the executive director.

The board's audit committee is chaired by a person with no financial relationship to the foundation outside that role. Any conflict of interest involving a board member or a member of staff must be disclosed.

The financial statements are audited annually by an independent external firm; the opinion is published in full, including any qualifications, should there be any.

What we do not do

We are not a medical institution and do not treat conditions requiring medication or hospitalisation — we refer, and we accompany the person through the referral.

We do not provide round-the-clock crisis care: that is what the helplines listed at the foot of every page are for.

We do not publish beneficiary stories without written, informed consent, and we do not use images that could identify a person, even where that would make the material more persuasive.
