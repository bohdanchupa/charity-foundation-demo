Foundation policy

Safeguarding from exploitation and abuse

UNIQ’s approach to protecting children and adults at risk and supporting survivors.

This expanded public version sets out the principles, responsibilities and core procedures of UNIQ International Charitable Foundation in plain language.

01

Purpose, scope and responsibility

This policy protects children and adults at risk from harm connected with UNIQ activities, personnel, partners or resources. It applies across programmes, locations, travel, online counselling, events, research, communications and assistance distribution.

Compliance is a condition of working with the Foundation. Managers are responsible for risk assessment and safe programme design; every team member must report concerns promptly; partners must maintain compatible safeguards, train their teams and notify UNIQ in time.

02

Zero tolerance and prohibited conduct

UNIQ prohibits sexual exploitation, abuse, harassment, ill-treatment, neglect, trafficking, coercion, humiliation and harmful punishment. Assistance must never be exchanged for money paid to staff or intermediaries, sex, personal services, labour or silence.

Sexual activity with anyone under 18 is prohibited regardless of local age-of-consent rules or mistaken belief about age. Intimate relationships with assistance recipients are unacceptable where unequal power exists. A suspected breach triggers immediate risk management and appropriate review.

03

Safe programme design and risk assessment

Before a programme starts, the team identifies who may be harmed, where one-to-one contact occurs, what data is collected, and how transport, spaces, queues, online contact and referrals are organised. Risks are reviewed when the location, team, partner or delivery method changes.

Controls must be practical: visible conduct rules, trained personnel, accessible complaints channels, safe space design, justified two-adult arrangements, controlled data access and verified referral services. If risk cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, the activity is changed or stopped.

04

Rules for working with children

Contact with children must reflect their age, development and best interests. Staff avoid unnecessary one-to-one situations, do not invite children to private homes, do not use personal channels for secret communication, and never use physical punishment, insults or manipulation.

Photos, video, stories and research participation require understandable consent from a legal guardian and informed assent from the child appropriate to their age. Information that reveals location or increases risk is not published. Refusal never affects access to assistance.

05

Survivor-centred response

The survivor’s safety, dignity, choice and wellbeing are the priority. We listen without blame, avoid leading questions, do not require repeated traumatic detail and never promise an outcome we cannot guarantee. Available options and limits of confidentiality are explained clearly.

Support decisions are made with the survivor as far as safe and lawful. Age, disability, language and cultural needs, gender and retaliation risk are considered. The person’s needs take priority over the organisation’s reputational interests.

06

Reporting and immediate action

Anyone may report a concern, disclosure or unsafe conduct through the reporting channel, a programme worker or designated officer. A staff member receiving a report records only essential facts, does not investigate independently and passes the information to the responsible person without delay.

Where life or health is in immediate danger, priorities are a safe place, urgent medical care and contact with appropriate services. Action is agreed with the survivor unless law or immediate risk requires otherwise.

07

Referral, support and confidentiality

With informed consent, UNIQ may arrange referral to medical, psychosocial, legal, social or other specialised support. Referral services are checked for accessibility, safety, competence and confidentiality. A referral does not mean automatic transfer of all information.

Records are stored separately, access follows a need-to-know principle and safe communication channels are used. People implicated in an allegation do not access case material beyond procedural need. Retaliation, pressure or attempts to force withdrawal of a report are prohibited.

08

Case review, action and learning

Reports are assessed for risk, conflicts of interest and the need for external expertise. Interim measures may include changed duties, restricted access or suspended contact without prejudging guilt. Review must be timely, impartial and documented.

Once facts are established, the Foundation takes proportionate disciplinary, contractual, protective, financial or legal action. Donors and competent authorities are notified where required. Lessons inform risk assessments, training, agreements and programme design without identifying the survivor.

09

Implementation, training and individual accountability

UNIQ management is responsible for turning this policy into clear procedures, roles, templates and control steps. Functional leads integrate the policy into plans, budgets, agreements and daily decisions rather than treating it as a formal document. Resources and authority must match the identified risks.

Staff, volunteers and others receive induction and periodic role-appropriate training, ask questions when a rule is unclear, and do not follow an instruction that plainly conflicts with policy or law. Acknowledgement and required training are recorded. Partners ensure compatible awareness within their teams and may receive practical support from UNIQ.

10

Monitoring, review and policy accessibility

The Foundation monitors implementation through relevant indicators, sample checks, reports, incidents, audit findings and feedback from staff, partners and programme participants. Monitoring should identify not only individual breaches but systemic causes such as unclear roles, excessive workload, inaccessible channels, weak agreements or controls that cannot work in practice.

The policy is reviewed regularly and after significant changes in law, structure, programmes, technology, donor requirements or a serious incident. The current public version is available in Ukrainian and English, and the team is informed of material changes. Earlier decisions are assessed against rules in force at the time unless law or protection of a person requires otherwise.
